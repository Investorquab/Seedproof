## Project page update

**Day 1 on testnet: a seed phrase thief, refused seven times**

Most vaults here survive a leaked key. Seedproof survives a leaked seed phrase too.

The problem: QoreX derives your post-quantum key from the same 24 words as your classical key. So the way people actually lose crypto (a phished or photographed seed phrase) hands a thief both keys.

What we shipped today on qorechain-diana:

- **An account that no longer trusts its own seed phrase.** We rotated a live account's ML-DSA-87 key to an independent Home Key on a second device (MsgRotatePQCKey). A send signed by the Home Key went through. The same send signed with the key from the 24 words was refused, `pqc` code 21.
- **Works for existing QoreX accounts.** Same result on a classic QoreX-style account: Home Key send accepted, seed thief refused.
- **A thief holding all 24 words tried everything:** send, send without a PQ signature, swap the key for his own, register a new key, open a wide EVM window, send through the open window, drain the vault. Every attempt was refused by the chain or reverted.
- **SeedproofVault is live** (`0xadB21AAC72079Ce455a0b09ee56f069bf5E4f27C`). Home Key signatures are checked on chain with `pqcVerify`. We ran a full cycle: a capped instant top-up, a refused over-limit top-up, and a withdrawal that waited its delay before it could execute.
- **Only scoped windows.** The Pocket opens QOR-only, vault-only or single-token windows, never an open-to-anything one.

Every transaction hash is in the repo: docs/TESTNET.md. The threat model (nine attacker cases, including what we cannot stop yet) is in docs/THREAT_MODEL.md.

Next: the vault screen in the Pocket app, Steal This Seed (we will publish the real 24 words of a funded account), and the QCAI audit.
